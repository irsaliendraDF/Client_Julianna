How these numbers are calculated

Cost per departed employee: Average annual salary multiplied by an industry-specific multiplier ranging from 1.5× to 1.8×. The multiplier reflects role complexity, ramp-up time, recruiting and onboarding costs, and lost productivity. Specifically: 1.5× for industrial, manufacturing, construction, and skilled trades; 1.6× for supply chain and logistics; 1.7× for energy, utilities, HR, and recruitment; 1.8× for engineering, tech, SaaS, and med tech. These ranges are consistent with widely cited turnover-cost research from SHRM and similar HR research bodies.

Annual turnover rate: Departures in the past year divided by total employees, expressed as a percentage. The 15 to 22% benchmark reflects typical ranges across industrial, construction, supply chain, energy, tech, healthcare, and recruitment sectors.

Productivity gap: Lost output during vacancy periods, calculated as (monthly salary × months to fill × number of departures).

Hidden burnout cost: Each selected signal carries a 5 to 10% cost multiplier against total payroll, layered with a sick-day cost (2 to 4% of payroll) and the cost of manager hours spent on disengagement.

10 year generational projection: Current annual cost × 10. Assumes the underlying drivers of turnover and burnout are not addressed and are compounded by generational workforce change.

Savings projections: Based on outcome ranges typically reported by structured workplace wellness programs (20 to 40% turnover reduction, 30 to 50% burnout cost reduction, 5 to 30% productivity gain from re-engagement).

Numbers are directional. Designed to surface the scale of the problem, not to replace a formal audit.
